# Against

The evidence against what the front page assumes, and the disagreements. Sources are in [review](review.md). None is deleted to make the subject look stronger.

**Shape:** [against](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/a7f405f26f14cd188291e2a86b630121c4302a94/shapes/against.md)

## Against "advice to seek grants and accelerators is common and the measured effect is a question"

- The question has partial answers, and they are positive for grants to small technology firms: Howell 2017 and Lerner 1996 both find gains (abstracts, secondary). Measured.
- The positive results come from one program, SBIR, and from firms that applied; they say little about a grant for a consumer product.

## Against "runway is a formal concept"

- Runway is not defined in any formal source found (F1). The review treats it as practice, and the program defines it from a month-by-month cash balance.
- The practitioners' guide gives burn and no runway formula (Andreessen 2015). Two practitioners can compute different runways from the same cash.

## Against "cash running out is the cause of failure"

- The count of 70% is of venture-backed companies that shut down, and the producer says running out of capital is usually the final cause, not the root (CB Insights 2026, extract, secondary). A small venture without venture backing is not in that sample.

## Disagreements

| Point | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Do public R&D subsidies add private R&D? | no substantial additionality after correcting bias (Dimos 2016, secondary) | an award doubles later venture capital and raises patenting (Howell 2017, secondary) |
| Does an accelerator help? | schooling bundled with services helps; services alone showed no effect (Gonzalez-Uribe 2017, secondary) | some accelerators substantially aid ventures, some do not (Hallen 2020, secondary) |
| Does subsidy work for all firms? | the effect exists solely for small firms (Criscuolo 2019, secondary) | heterogeneity that methods do not explain (Zuniga-Vicente 2012, secondary) |

- A possible reason for the first split: the studies measure different outcomes (private R&D spending, later funding) and different programs.
- A possible reason for the second: the programs differ in what they bundle.

## Against the sources themselves

- Four of the 25 formal works were read in a search summary and six were located only; most figures are secondary.
- The grey sources are tiers 1 to 3 from one reviewer; the largest count (70%) is one producer's.
- The sources on funding are mostly American or Chilean; none was found for a small European venture.
- No source here tests a runway forecast against what happened.
