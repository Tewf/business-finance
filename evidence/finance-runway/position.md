# Position

Step 4 of the review. What is known, what was not found, and what this repository asks. Each "not found" is a statement about these searches, not about the world. Terms are in [frame](frame.md).

**Shape:** [position](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics-review/blob/a7f405f26f14cd188291e2a86b630121c4302a94/shapes/position.md)

## Known

- Break-even is a settled definition (OpenStax 2026). Burn is a practitioners' definition (Andreessen 2015).
- Most small firms are limited by internal finance (Carpenter 2002).
- Staged release of money and bootstrapping are the studied forms of paying late (Gompers 1995; Winborg 2001).
- Grants and accelerators have measured effects that depend on the program and what it bundles (Howell 2017; Gonzalez-Uribe 2017; Dimos 2016).
- Plans are optimistic, and an average hides the range (Buehler 1994; Savage 2002).

## Not found in these searches

- A formal definition of runway (F1).
- A study that checks a cash forecast of a small venture against what happened (F5).
- An open program that computes runway from the events that open each cost (G1 to G3).
- A measured effect of event-driven cost discipline as such. Its nearest studied forms are staging and bootstrapping.

## The questions

| Question | Answer from the evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 How long does cash last when revenue comes late? | as long as the costs paid before the first sale allow; no formula is standard, so the program defines one | convention |
| Q2 What does each source cost? | bootstrapping costs control of growth, staged equity costs the option to stop, headline valuations overstate | supported with caveat |
| Q3 What do grants and accelerators return? | positive for SBIR-type grants to technology firms, mixed for subsidies in general and accelerators | measured, mixed |
| Q4 Which faults can a program find? | cost without an event, cash ending before an event, no break-even, a state that cannot open; their frequency is unmeasured | convention |

## What this repository asks

- Does a runway computed from the events that open each cost differ from the spreadsheet rule, and by how much on real plans?
- How often do plans carry the faults the program finds?
- This is a question, not a claim that nobody has asked it: the searches were a first pass.

## What the shapes must decide

- Whether the program stays a check of a plan or becomes a model.
- Whether a Monte Carlo run over the provisional values is worth adding to the three scenarios.
- Whether the repository is renamed after the field's name, entrepreneurial finance.
