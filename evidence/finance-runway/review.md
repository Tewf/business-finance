## Strand 0: existing reviews

- Zuniga-Vicente 2012 finds a considerable heterogeneity of results on public R&D subsidies that methods do not explain (abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Dimos 2016 pools 52 evaluations since 2000 and, after correcting for publication bias, rejects crowding out but finds no substantial additionality (summary, secondary). Measured.
- Berger 1998 reviews small business finance and states that different capital structures are optimal at different points of a financial growth cycle (abstract, secondary). Supported with caveat.
- Gompers 2001 reviews venture capital; its abstract names the mechanisms used at each stage of investment (abstract, secondary). Convention.
- Crisan 2019 is a systematic review of accelerators; it was located and not read.
- No review of runway or of cash forecasts for small ventures was found (protocol, string F1).

## Strand 1: runway and break-even (Q1)

- Break-even is the volume at which total cost equals total revenue; in units it is fixed costs divided by the contribution margin per unit (OpenStax 2026, text extract, secondary). Convention.
- Burn rate is the rate at which cash falls; net burn is revenue less gross burn, and investors read it to judge how long cash lasts. The guide gives no runway formula (Andreessen 2015, extract, secondary). Convention, untested.
- Founders should ask whether, with expenses constant and growth as it has been, they reach profit on the money left (Graham 2015, read in full). He states that half the founders he talks to do not know. Convention, untested.
- Of 385 failed venture-backed companies whose reason was identified, 70% cited running out of capital; the page adds that it is usually the final cause and not the root (CB Insights 2026, extract, secondary). Measured, descriptive, one producer.
- In a panel of more than 1,600 small firms, growth of most firms was constrained by internal finance (Carpenter 2002, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- A cost recovered slowly strains cash: a subscription firm that pays up front to acquire customers has a trough that deepens with faster growth (Skok 2020, extract). Example of practice only.
- No formal source defining runway was found in these searches (F1). Not found, in these searches.

## Strand 2: funding and event-driven cost (Q2)

- Staged finance lets the investor keep the option to stop funding projects with little chance of success; staging concentrates in early-stage, high-technology firms (Gompers 1995, abstract, 794 firms, secondary). Measured.
- Effectuation reasons from control, not prediction (Sarasvathy 2001, abstract, secondary). Convention. The nearest formal name to "nothing is paid before its event" is affordable loss, a part of that theory; the abstract does not use the term, so the link is the reviewer's.
- Small business managers use bootstrapping instead of external finance; a survey of 900 managers built on 32 methods (Winborg 2001, summary, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Different bootstrapping types are used at different periods of a firm's life (Ebben 2006, summary, secondary). Measured, descriptive.
- Start-up financing varies with size, asset structure, organization type and growth orientation (Cassar 2004, summary, secondary). Measured.
- Angel funding is correlated with survival and growth gains of 30% to 50%; a regression discontinuity keeps a positive effect on survival and growth but not on follow-on funding (Kerr 2010, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Reported valuations of 135 unicorns average 50% above fair value, so a round's headline price is not the cash it buys (Gornall 2017, abstract, secondary). Measured.

## Strand 3: grants and accelerators (Q3)

- An award of the U.S. Department of Energy SBIR grant approximately doubles the probability of later venture capital, with large positive effects on patenting and commercialization, stronger for constrained firms (Howell 2017, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- SBIR awardees grew significantly faster than a matched set over ten years (Lerner 1996, abstract, secondary). Measured, matched not randomized.
- The effect was confined to firms in zip codes with much venture capital activity, and multiple awards added nothing (Lerner 1996, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- A 10-percentage-point rise in the maximum investment subsidy raised manufacturing employment by 10%; the effect exists solely for small firms (Criscuolo 2019, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Start-Up Chile: schooling bundled with basic services significantly raised performance; funding and coworking space alone showed no evidence of an effect (Gonzalez-Uribe 2017, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Some, not all, of the early accelerators studied substantially aided venture development; sorting also appeared (Hallen 2020, abstract, secondary). Measured.
- Yu 2016 and Cohen 2019 study accelerators; they were located and not read.

## Strand 4: scenarios, forecasts and the program (Q4)

- People's predictions of completion times were too optimistic across tasks; the bias vanished when they were told to connect plans to relevant past experience (Buehler 1994, abstract, study 4, secondary). Measured.
- Plans built on average conditions usually fail: the average hides the range (Savage 2002, extract, secondary). Convention.
- Judgmental forecasts and prediction intervals are standard topics of forecasting practice (Hyndman 2021, table of contents). Convention.
- Entrepreneurs' perceived chances of success were studied by Cooper 1988; it was located and not read.
- No open program for an event-driven runway was found: three repository searches returned calculators of other kinds with few stars (G1 to G3). Not found, in these searches.
